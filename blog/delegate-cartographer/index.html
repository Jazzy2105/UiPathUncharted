UiPath Delegate & Cartographer

One agent does the work on your desktop. The other writes down how the work actually runs — exceptions and judgment calls included. No CLI here: Cartographer lives inside the UiPath desktop app, and you build a project by hand. Here's every setting, screen by screen.

Jarryd Scott Technical Lead, Business Automation · host of UiPath Uncharted
Ep. 03 companion

Two products, one app

Both went generally available at FUSION 2026. They install alongside UiPath Assistant and you move between the three from the switcher in the title bar. Cartographer is built on Delegate, but they answer different questions.

  • Acts

    UiPath Delegate

    A desktop agent that does the work you'd otherwise do by hand. You describe the task in plain language and it drives your apps, documents and business systems, under your organisation's existing controls.

    • input · a plain-language ask or a recording
    • output · the task, done, or a reusable routine
    • best for · repetitive personal desktop work

  • Maps

    UiPath Cartographer

    An AI agent for business analysts. It turns transcripts, interviews, SOPs and recordings into governed process documentation: a PDD, then an SDD once the PDD is approved.

    • input · SOPs, recordings, SME interviews
    • output · AS-IS / TO-BE, PDD, SDD
    • best for · processes you're about to automate

Why no uip commands?

There's no uip CLI skill for Delegate or Cartographer. You set up a Cartographer project in the app itself, and the uip CLI only comes in later, once the SDD has been sent to Automation Hub.

Setup 1 · Project basics

In Cartographer, start a new business process. The New project dialog has three tabs down the left: Basics, Allowlist and Skills. Basics is the only one you must fill in. Click a number to see what each field does.

  1. →

    A stronger project brief

    copy & adapt

    The default instructions work. Being specific about scope and sources gets you a sharper first draft and fewer clarifying questions.

    Project instructions
    › You are documenting a business process for automation.
    › Goal: capture the AS-IS, design the TO-BE, generate the PDD.
    › Scope: AP invoice exceptions only, from receipt to posting in SAP.
    › Treat the attached SOP as intent, not truth. Flag every
    › place where the recordings disagree with it.
    › Audience: CoE solution architects. Use our system names.
    Allowlist

    The Allowlist tab lists the local files and folders Cartographer may use when Guarded mode is on, plus linked project resources that stay in sync with their source. Point it at the process's folder and nothing else.

Setup 2 · Preload skills

Skills are packaged know-how the agent can draw on. This tab decides which ones are already loaded when a conversation starts, so the agent doesn't have to discover them first. Changes only apply to new conversations: a session keeps the skills it started with.

The project home

Click Create and you land on the project's home page. It has three columns: navigation on the left, the conversation in the middle, and the project's standing context on the right.

Your first session

Everything from here on is conversation. These are the prompts I'd actually type, in order.

  1. 01

    Open with the AS-IS

    ~15 min

    Click Document AS-IS, or just describe the process. Cartographer interviews you step by step and asks about triggers, systems, volumes and the exceptions nobody wrote down.

    Cartographer — prompt
    › AP invoice exceptions. Invoices that fail 3-way match land
    › in the AP mailbox; an analyst checks SAP, emails the buyer,
    › and posts or rejects. ~400 a month, one analyst covers it.
    Beginner tip

    Answer with what really happens, not what the SOP says. The contradictions between the two are what the documentation is for.

  2. 02

    Feed it a recording

    ~10 min

    Use Create from recording to capture one real run, or attach an existing recording. Cartographer turns what it sees into confirmed steps on the AS-IS map.

    Cartographer — prompt
    › Here's a recording of me handling one price-variance
    › exception end to end. Update the AS-IS and list anything
    › that contradicts the attached SOP.
  3. 03

    Design the TO-BE

    ~15 min

    Cartographer goes through the steps one by one and asks how each will be handled: Fully Automated, Assisted, Agent, Human Review or Manual. It then draws a future-state map colour-coded by mode. Confirming that map is a mandatory checkpoint.

    Cartographer — prompt
    › Move to TO-BE. Mark anything you're unsure of as Manual
    › and provisional. Approvals over the threshold are Human Review.
    Conservative default

    If you're unsure about a step, choose Manual and mark it provisional. You can change any mode before or after the PDD is generated.

  4. 04

    Generate the PDD, then the SDD

    ~10 min

    Once the TO-BE is confirmed, Cartographer generates the PDD and works through the quality checklist with you. When the PDD is approved, it produces the SDD for the build team and their coding agents.

    Cartographer — prompt
    › Generate the PDD. Then walk me through every open item
    › on the checklist before we produce the SDD.
    Handoff

    From the SDD you can send everything to Automation Hub, and that's where the uip CLI and coding agents from Episodes 1 and 2 take over.

Where it breaks

Go in with eyes open on three things. First, the output is only as good as the interview. Cartographer drafts, but it never approves, so a rushed AS-IS gives you a confident, wrong PDD. Second, Full access approval mode on a real project is a governance conversation, not a personal preference. Your admin can enforce limits through Automation Ops policies. Third, licensing: ask your account team in writing what Delegate and Cartographer sessions consume.
