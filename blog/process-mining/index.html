Process Mining: mine the mess before you automate it

Everyone said vendor onboarding takes about a week. 1,800 cases and 257 variants later, the data disagreed. This is a complete, hands-on walkthrough of mining a process with UiPath Process Mining and the uip CLI, from CSV to root cause, mistakes included.

Jarryd Scott Technical Lead, Business Automation · host of UiPath Uncharted
Hands-on deep dive

Mine it before you automate it

Every automation programme has the same bad habit. Somebody describes a process in a workshop, we draw it on a whiteboard as five tidy boxes, and then we automate the whiteboard. Six weeks later the bot is live and the process is still slow, because the whiteboard was never the process.

Process mining fixes that by refusing to listen to anyone. It reads the timestamps your systems already write and rebuilds what actually happened, case by case: every path, every loop, every wait. It is the last stop in the Discover layer for a reason. Automation Hub collects the ideas, the Automation Discovery Skill finds the ones nobody mentioned, Delegate and Cartographer document how people do the work, and process mining proves what the work really looks like at scale.

  • You think

    The whiteboard

    Request comes in, details captured, checks run, compliance approves, vendor created. Five boxes, about a week.

  • The data says

    The process

    1,800 cases took 257 different paths. The most common path covers only 27% of them. Some cases go round the same loop three times.

  • So you

    Automate the right thing

    Fix the loop that costs 6 days per case before you build a robot that does the five boxes faster.

Where this fits in the series

This post closes the Discover pillar, and it hands straight over to Maestro Case Management in Run & Scale. Same process, vendor onboarding. Here we find out what is wrong with it; there we rebuild it as a case.

The use case: Karoo Distribution

Meet Karoo Distribution, a made-up South African distributor that onboards around 300 new suppliers a month. Requests arrive three ways: an email to Procurement, a phone call that someone writes up, or a web form. Three clerks capture the details, run a duplicate check, verify the company registration and bank account, and send the vendor through sanctions screening. A risk model scores the vendor, compliance signs off, and the vendor is created in SAP with a welcome pack.

Everyone agrees it takes "about a week". The CFO wants to know why some take three. That is our question.

About the data

Karoo Distribution is fictional and the event log is synthetic: I wrote a small Python generator so there is no client data anywhere near a public blog. Everything after that is real. The app, the ingestion, the SQL, the errors, the numbers and every screenshot come from a real UiPath Process Mining app on my dev tenant, built with the uipath-process-mining skill. I did plant a few problems in the generator, the way they show up in real onboarding data. The fun part is watching the tooling find them without being told where to look.

Vendor request received

From email (59%), phone (16%) or web form (25%). The web form forces the vendor to upload documents up front. Email and phone do not.

  • 3 channels
  • Requester or vendor portal

Details captured and checked for duplicates

A clerk types the vendor into the onboarding tracker and checks it does not already exist. Sometimes the details are wrong and get corrected later.

  • 3 clerks
  • Duplicate check

Registration, bank account, sanctions

Three checks, usually in that order but not always. If documents are missing, the clerk emails the vendor, waits, sends reminders, and verifies the bank account again when they arrive.

  • Rework loop
  • Reminders

Risk scored, compliance decides

A risk model scores the vendor. Compliance approves or rejects. High-risk vendors can be escalated to the Head of Procurement first.

  • 2 reviewers
  • Escalations

Created in SAP, welcome pack sent

The vendor is created in SAP and gets a welcome pack. Occasionally, weeks later, somebody changes the vendor's bank details. Finance should be nervous about that.

  • SAP
  • Bank changes

An event log in three columns

Process mining needs exactly three things per row: which case, what happened and when. Everything else is a bonus. Here is the top of the real file, 21,385 rows of it:

vendor_onboarding_events.csv
Case_ID;Activity;Event_end;User;Case;Case_status;Case_type;Case_value;Channel;Region;Department
VEND-27095;Vendor request received;2026-01-05 11:11:07;Requester;Waterberg Consulting Holdings;Onboarded;Services;946000;Email;Eastern Cape;Fleet
VEND-27039;Vendor request received;2026-01-05 14:14:23;Requester;Vaal IT Solutions Trading;Onboarded;Services;393000;Email;Gauteng;IT
VEND-26888;Vendor request received;2026-01-06 08:10:28;Requester;Namaqua Print CC;Onboarded;Goods;107000;Email;Eastern Cape;Marketing

  • Required

    Case_ID · Activity · Event_end

    The case key, the step name and its timestamp. With only these three you already get the process graph, variants and throughput times.

  • Template hooks

    Case · Case_status · Case_type · Case_value

    The uipath.custom template's Cases.sql looks for these four by name. Name your columns to match and you skip the most common first-run error (more on that below).

  • Bonus

    Channel · Region · Department · User

    Attributes you want to slice by. They load even if you do not map them, but you have to promote them to case level before you can group by them.

Real systems

In real life the event log comes from SAP change documents, a ServiceNow audit table or an Orchestrator queue. For a source system with a full extract, use the matching template (for example uipath.p2p.sap). For one flat log like this, use uipath.custom, the "Event log" template.

Meet the skill: uipath-process-mining

The skill teaches your coding agent to drive uip pm, the Process Mining part of the UiPath CLI, from a raw CSV to a queryable app. Before the hands-on part, here are the rules it lives by. Each one saved me (or would have saved me) a slow round trip.

Seven rules · click a row

  • Every template shares the same pipeline: mapping → upload → ingest → transform → query. Only the expected input tables differ. A single denormalised log is uipath.custom. A full SAP or ServiceNow extract gets its own template.

  • The event-log template's Cases.sql references Case, Case_status, Case_type and Case_value. A minimal mapping does not produce them and dbt fails with 000904 invalid identifier. Either null them in SQL or, like me, include them in the CSV.

  • A transformation error means the data loaded fine. Fix the SQL, push it, and run transformations apply. Re-ingesting only helps when the raw file or the mapping changed.

  • apps data-mapping get, edit, update --etag, then re-ingest. Recreating the app throws away every transformation you already wrote.

  • ingestions create --wait and transformations apply --wait block until done and print the dbt or loader error if it fails.

  • Query bodies take hashed ids like F__Cases__Channel__7fdf…. The --group-by / --metric sugar resolves plain names for you. And you can only group by case-level fields, not event-level ones.

  • Iterate on the dev stage. Publish when the model is right. The published stage then needs its own ingestion before dashboards show data.

Hands-on: from CSV to a mined process

This is the exact path I took on my dev tenant, mistakes included. I had the skill loaded in Claude Code and let it drive. Every command below is one it ran, and every output is real. Budget an hour the first time.

You need The UiPath CLI (uip) 1.203+ A coding agent with the UiPath skills A tenant with Process Mining enabled An event log as CSV
  1. 01

    Check the skill is installed

    ~2 min

    If you followed the Case Management post, you already have it: uip skills install installs the whole catalog. Confirm it is there.

    Terminal
    $ uip skills install --agent claude
    $ uip skills show uipath-process-mining
    # the commands it drives
    $ uip pm --help
    Commands

    uip pm has groups for apps, files, ingestions, transformations, query, conformance, deviations and simulations. You will use the first five today.

  2. 02

    Log in to the right tenant

    ~2 min

    This one caught me. My organisation has a Development and a Production tenant, and a plain login stopped with an error because I had not said which. Name the tenant explicitly, then check before you create anything.

    Terminal
    $ uip login
    ConfigError: Multiple tenants available in organization "…"; none selected.
    $ uip login --tenant <your-dev-tenant>
    $ uip login status --output json
    Security

    The CLI login is separate from UiPath Assistant and Studio. Being signed in there does not mean the CLI is pointing at the same place. Always check login status before you write to a tenant.

  3. 03

    Pre-flight the CSV

    ~5 min

    Each check here saves a multi-minute ingestion that fails. The skill runs these locally before anything leaves your machine: encoding, delimiter, a regular field count on every line, no empty trailing rows, and the date format.

    What to check
    # UTF-8, ";" delimited, 11 fields on every line
    # dates are yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm:ss (not dd-mm, not mm-dd)
    # 1,800 distinct Case_IDs, 20 distinct activities
    ✓ 21,385 rows · no empty rows · no embedded delimiters
    Date formats

    If the first number in a date ever goes above 12, it is day-first. Process Mining wants the format spelled out, lowercase and not strftime: dd-mm-yyyy hh:mm:ss.

  4. 04

    Write the mapping

    ~5 min

    The mapping tells the loader which source column becomes which target field. Map the three required ones (plus User) and leave the rest: unmapped columns still load under their own names.

    mapping.json
    { "Tables": [ {
    "SourceName": "Event_log", "TargetName": "Event_log", "Source": "blob",
    "SourceSettings": { "Encoding": "utf-8", "FieldDelimiter": ";", "QuoteCharacter": "\"" },
    "Fields": [
    { "DataType": "text", "SourceName": "Case_ID", "TargetName": "Case_ID", "IsMandatory": true },
    { "DataType": "text", "SourceName": "Activity", "TargetName": "Activity", "IsMandatory": true },
    { "DataType": "datetime", "SourceName": "Event_end", "TargetName": "Event_end", "IsMandatory": true,
    "DataTypeSettings": { "DateTimeFormatString": "yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm:ss" } },
    { "DataType": "text", "SourceName": "User", "TargetName": "User" }
    ] } ] }
  5. 05

    Create the app, upload, ingest

    ~10 min

    Three commands. apps create builds the app from the template, files upload sends the CSV, and ingestions create --wait loads it and runs the transformations.

    My first ingestion bounced: the app was still provisioning. Wait a minute and run it again.

    Terminal
    $ uip pm app-types list --output-filter "[].{Key:AppTypeKey,Name:DefaultName}"
    $ uip pm apps create "UU Vendor Onboarding Mining" --type uipath.custom --data-mapping ./mapping.json
    $ uip pm files upload $app ./vendor_onboarding_events.csv --input-table Event_log
    $ uip pm ingestions create $app --file-format csv --field-delimiter ";" --encoding utf-8 --wait
    ✓ Status: SUCCESS_WITH_WARNINGS
    Warnings are fine

    SUCCESS_WITH_WARNINGS on a first ingest usually means optional template columns you did not provide. Because my CSV already had Case, Case_status, Case_type and Case_value, the famous Cases.sql error never happened.

  6. 06

    See what you can query

    ~3 min

    query info lists every queryable table and field. Here is the catch: Channel, Region and Department landed on the events table, not on Cases. And query run can only group by case-level fields. Grouping by an event field fails with UserError_EventTableUsedInQuery.

    Terminal
    $ uip pm query info $app --output json
    Cases Case_ID · Case · Case_status · Case_type · Case_value · Event_count · throughput …
    Event_log Activity · Event_end · User · Channel · Region · Department …
  7. 07

    Promote attributes to the case

    ~10 min

    So we edit the dbt model that builds Cases. Pull it, add the attributes, and while we are there, add flags we will want for root-cause analysis: did this case need documents chased, did the bank details change after creation, who reviewed it and how long compliance took.

    Terminal
    $ uip pm transformations get $app models/Cases.sql --destination Cases.sql
    # note the ETag it returns - the update must send the same one
    models/Cases.sql (my version)
    select
    Event_log."Case_ID",
    min(Event_log."Case") as "Case",
    min(Event_log."Case_status") as "Case_status",
    min(Event_log."Case_type") as "Case_type",
    min(Event_log."Case_value") as "Case_value",
    min(Event_log."Channel") as "Channel",
    min(Event_log."Region") as "Region",
    min(Event_log."Department") as "Department",
    count_if(Event_log."Activity" = 'Documents requested from vendor') as "Document_requests",
    iff(count_if(Event_log."Activity" = 'Documents requested from vendor') > 0, 'Yes', 'No') as "Missing_documents",
    iff(count_if(Event_log."Activity" = 'Bank details changed after creation') > 0, 'Yes', 'No') as "Bank_changed_after_creation",
    max(iff(Event_log."Activity" in ('Compliance approved', 'Compliance rejected', 'Escalated to Head of Procurement'), Event_log."User", null)) as "Compliance_reviewer",
    round(datediff('minute', <risk assessed>, <first compliance decision>) / 1440.0, 1) as "Compliance_wait_days",
    count(*) as "Event_count"
    from {{ source('sources', 'Event_log') }} as Event_log
    group by Event_log."Case_ID"
    Terminal
    $ uip pm transformations update $app models/Cases.sql --file Cases.sql --etag '<etag from get>'
    $ uip pm transformations apply $app --wait
    ETags

    The update must carry the ETag from your get. If someone else changed the file in between, you get a 409. The fix is to get it again and re-apply your change, not to grab a fresh ETag and overwrite them.

  8. 08

    Ask it questions

    ~10 min

    Now the new case fields are queryable straight away. The --group-by and --metric sugar takes plain names. Throughput comes back in milliseconds, so 789,227,782 is 9.1 days.

    Terminal
    $ uip pm query run $app --group-by Channel --metric Case_ID:count:Cases --metric $T:average:Avg_throughput --output table
    $ uip pm query run $app --group-by Missing_documents --metric Case_ID:count:Cases --metric $T:average:Avg_throughput --output table
    $ uip pm query percentile $app --field $T --values 0.5,0.9,0.95
    { "Values": [ 628123750, 1218046000, 1443483000 ] } # 7.3 · 14.1 · 16.7 days
  9. 09

    Root-cause analysis

    ~5 min

    query rca takes a selected set of cases and tells you which attribute values are over-represented in it. My first try selected cases by Document_requests in [1,2,3] and failed with UserError_UnsupportedFilterTypeKind: value lists only work on text fields. That is why the SQL above has a Yes/No Missing_documents flag.

    rca.json
    { "argument": "Case_ID",
    "exploreAttributes": ["<Channel id>", "Case_type", "<Region id>", "<Department id>"],
    "selectedSet": [[{ "kind": "values", "dimension": "<Missing_documents id>",
    "type": "string", "values": ["Yes"], "invert": false, "displayType": "list" }]] }
    Terminal
    $ uip pm query rca $app --body rca.json
    Reading RCA

    The response is columnar: Total, Selected, Percentage, Attribute and Value arrays, index-aligned. Pivot it into rows (I had Claude do it) and sort by percentage. The interactive version is in the next section.

  10. 10

    Publish, and load the published stage

    ~10 min

    Publishing copies your transformations to the published stage that dashboards and colleagues use. It reports IngestionNeeded: true. My first published ingestion failed with "Mandatory Table 'Event_log' is missing": each stage has its own data, so upload the file to the published stage as well.

    Terminal
    $ uip pm apps publish $app
    { "Changes": { "Transformations": true, "Bpmn": true }, "IngestionNeeded": true }
    $ uip pm files upload $app ./vendor_onboarding_events.csv --input-table Event_log --stage published
    $ uip pm ingestions create $app --stage published --file-format csv --field-delimiter ";" --encoding utf-8 --wait
    ✓ Status: SUCCESS_WITH_WARNINGS
  11. 11

    Open the dashboards

    ~5 min

    Open Process Mining in Automation Cloud and the app is on the home page: 1.80K cases and 21.39K events. Everything you built from the terminal is now a set of dashboards anyone with access can use, no CLI needed.

Reading the process graph

This is the moment process mining earns its keep. Open End-to-end and expand the process graph. Thick lines are common paths, thin ones are rare, and the numbers on every arrow are case counts. Take a minute with it before you read on.

Three things jump out once you know where to look:

  • Left side

    The documents loop

    711 cases went Documents requested → Reminder → Documents received → Bank account verified at least once. 498 reminders went out along the way. That loop is the single biggest source of delay.

  • Middle

    Bank verification, again

    Bank account verified happens 1.57 times per case on average. Every documents loop forces a re-check. Process Insights flags this as rework without being asked.

  • Bottom

    Bank details changed after creation

    106 vendors had their bank details changed after they were set up in SAP. That is not slow, it is risky: it is the classic pattern behind supplier payment fraud.

Test yourself before we go deeper:

Q1Most cases follow the most common path. True or false?

Variant 1 covers 490 of 1,800 cases, about 27%. The other 73% are spread over 256 other paths. That is normal, and it is why you never automate the whiteboard.

Q2Which activity does Process Insights flag as the top rework issue?

It happens 1.57 times per case, and the 11.67% of cases that repeat it take 15.3 days on average, 6.93 days more than the rest.

Q3You change the SQL in Cases.sql. What do you run next?

The data is already loaded. Apply re-runs the transformations over it. Re-ingest only when the raw file or the mapping changes.

Pick an answer for each one.

Process Insights: the analyst you did not hire

Before I had written a single query, the app had already found the problems. Process Insights scans every activity for rework, unusual throughput times and other anomalies, ranks them, and links each one straight to root-cause analysis. It found 23.

Why this matters for discovery

In a real project you do not know where the problems are, which is the whole point. Process Insights gives you a ranked shortlist in minutes, with the number of cases affected and the days each one costs. That is your automation pipeline's evidence, ready for Automation Hub.

Root cause: why do documents go missing?

Knowing that 711 cases looped is half the story. RCA tells us which cases. Below are the real results for two questions. Each bar shows how often the problem happens in that segment; the top three are highlighted.

    The answer is not subtle. Contractors and phone or email requests need documents chased about half the time. Web-form requests need it 15% of the time, because the form will not submit without the documents. And the risky bank-detail changes cluster on email requests, where the bank letter arrives as an attachment someone has to retype.

    The honest caveat

    RCA shows correlation, not cause. Here the story holds up because the mechanism is obvious: the web form validates documents up front. In real data, take an RCA finding back to the people doing the work before you build a business case on it.

    What the data says

    Put the queries, the graph and the insights together and Karoo Distribution's "one-week process" looks like this. Every number is from the app.

    Five findings · click a row

    • Cases that chase documents average 11.9 days against 6.0 for cases that do not. Over six months that is about 4,200 case-days lost to waiting for paperwork.

    • Email (1,061 cases) and phone (296) average about 9.1 days; the web form (443) averages 6.1. Web-form cases average 0.20 document requests; email and phone about 0.65.

    • Thandi reviews 70% of cases and they wait 2.0 days for a decision; Sipho's 508 wait 1.1 days. Nothing is wrong with Thandi. The queue is.

    • 83 of the 106 came in by email. Changing a vendor's bank account after onboarding should trigger a call-back, and right now nothing makes that happen.

    • Not a separate problem: it is what the documents loop looks like from the bank-verification step. Fix finding 1 and this one shrinks with it.

    What if we fixed the front door?

    The cheapest fix is the boring one: route email and phone requests through the web form, so documents are validated before a clerk ever sees the request. Drag the slider to see what the mined averages say that is worth.

    Requests moved (of 1,357)679

    Document chasers avoided310

    Average throughput 7.2 days
    Case-days saved per half-year 2,067

    Read this honestly

    This is back-of-envelope, using the real averages from the app: 9.1 days for email and phone, 6.1 for web form, over 1,800 cases in six months. It assumes moved requests behave like today's web-form requests. That is the right first guess, and exactly what UiPath Process Mining's Simulation module (in the same app) lets you model properly.

    From insight to automation

    This is where Discover hands over to Build and Run. Each finding maps to a fix, and most fixes are not a robot.

    • Finding 1 + 2

      Validate at the front door

      A UiPath App (or the existing web form) as the only way in, with document upload required. An agent can read inbound emails and reply with the form link plus a list of what is missing.

    • Finding 1 + 3

      Run it as a case

      Model the process as a Maestro case: a Missing Information lane that returns to Verification, an SLA on Risk Review, and compliance tasks shared across a group queue instead of one person.

    • Finding 4

      Control bank changes

      Any bank-detail change after creation starts an approval task and a call-back to a known number. Automation is not the point here; the control is.

    • Then

      Measure again

      Keep the app connected. Process mining is not a one-off audit: re-run the ingestion monthly and watch the documents loop shrink, or not.

    Communications Mining: the other half of the story

    Process mining reads systems. But look at where the vendor onboarding pain starts: an email to Procurement with "please set up this supplier" and three attachments, two of them the wrong ones. That is unstructured, and process mining cannot see inside it.

    UiPath Communications Mining is built for that side. It reads email, chat and ticket text, learns your taxonomy (request type, missing document, urgency, bank-change request) from a few hundred labelled examples, and turns a shared mailbox into structured, countable data. Point it at the Procurement mailbox and you could answer the questions the event log cannot: how many requests arrive without a bank letter, how many emails are vendors chasing status, and how many "please update our banking details" messages arrive outside the formal process.

    • Process Mining

      What happened, and when

      Reads system events. Finds the loops, waits and variants. Answers "where does the time go?"

    • Communications Mining

      What people are asking for

      Reads messages. Finds the intents, the missing information and the hidden requests. Answers "why did this case start, and what is it waiting for?"

    Where it breaks

    • Data

      Garbage timestamps

      Process mining is only as good as the clock. Batch jobs that stamp every event at midnight, or a day-first date read as month-first, produce a confident and completely wrong graph.

    • Scope

      The case key

      Pick the wrong case ID (the invoice instead of the vendor, the ticket instead of the customer) and every variant and throughput time is measured against the wrong thing.

    • Model

      Event fields vs case fields

      If you cannot group by something, it is probably on the event table. Promote it in Cases.sql and apply.

    • Lifecycle

      Two stages, two datasets

      Dev and published each have their own data. Publishing moves the logic, not the rows. Upload and ingest the published stage too.

    • People

      Mining is not monitoring people

      The reviewer finding is about a queue, not a person. Share process data the way you would want yours shared, and talk to the team before the slide deck does.

    • Display

      Units and currency

      Throughput comes back in milliseconds from the API, and currency defaults to $. Fix field kinds with apps model fields set, and check every comparison uses the same kind.
